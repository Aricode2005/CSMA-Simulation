Experiment 1: Effect of Probability (p) on p-Persistent CSMA
Objective

Evaluate how the transmission probability p in p-Persistent CSMA affects network performance metrics: Throughput, Average Delay, and Collisions.

Setup

A fixed network of N = 10 stations shares a single bus-topology channel. Each station has 100 frames to transmit. The probability p is swept from 0.01 to 1.0. When a station senses the channel idle, it transmits immediately with probability p, or defers for one time slot with probability (1-p). If the channel becomes busy during the deferred slot, it is treated as a collision and the station enters Binary Exponential Backoff.

Expected Observation

At very low p, throughput is low because stations defer too conservatively, wasting idle channel time. As p increases, throughput peaks at an optimal value. Beyond the optimum, throughput declines as p → 1.0 because stations transmit too aggressively (behaving like 1-Persistent CSMA), causing excessive collisions. Note: p = 1.0 is mathematically identical to 1-Persistent CSMA.

Experiment 2: Comparing All CSMA Strategies Under Increasing Load
Objective

Compare the performance of all four MAC strategies — Non-Persistent, 1-Persistent, p-Persistent (p=0.1), and CSMA/CD — as the number of competing stations on the shared bus increases from 2 to 30.

Setup

The number of stations N is varied across values: 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30. Each station sends 100 frames. The experiment is repeated for each of the four strategies. CSMA/CD uses 1-Persistent carrier sensing combined with early collision detection and abort (transmitting a JAM signal for 2 slots upon detection), followed by Binary Exponential Backoff with Kmax = 15.

Expected Observation

Non-Persistent CSMA yields the highest throughput under heavy load because stations back off for a random duration when the channel is busy, naturally staggering retransmission attempts and reducing collision probability. 1-Persistent CSMA is the most aggressive strategy and causes the most collisions as N grows, since all waiting stations transmit simultaneously the instant the channel becomes idle. CSMA/CD dramatically reduces wasted transmission time by aborting collisions early (within 1-2 slots) instead of blindly transmitting for the full 20-slot frame duration, yielding significantly better throughput than pure 1-Persistent CSMA. p-Persistent CSMA performance falls between Non-Persistent and 1-Persistent, depending on the chosen p value.
